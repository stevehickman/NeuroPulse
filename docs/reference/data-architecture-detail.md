# Data architecture — boundary resolutions, predictive maintenance, anonymization

> Relocated from CLAUDE.md §5.1 (the specific boundary-resolution list), §5.2 and §5.3 (Rev 40), and
> from §5.1's two full contents enumerations (Rev 43), to slim the always-loaded core. Content is
> verbatim; section numbers are unchanged. CLAUDE.md §5 keeps the UHDR/SHDR definitions, the
> defining tests, the when-in-doubt rule, the conditional-redaction rule and a representative
> handful of each contents list — the parts that decide most classification questions without this
> file.
>
> **Read this file when:** classifying a new telemetry field, touching the SHDR schema or fleet DB,
> working on predictive maintenance, or implementing the research anonymization pipeline.
>
> **The list below is authoritative per field and is the record `scripts/check-redaction-shape.ts`
> enforces the shape of.** A field not on it is decided by CLAUDE.md §5.1's defining test, and the
> answer is added here.

### 5.1 Contents of each record — full enumeration (relocated from §5.1, Rev 43)

CLAUDE.md §5.1 keeps each record's owner, access rules, defining test, storage and the two general
rules, plus a representative handful of each contents list. The complete enumerations are here.

**UHDR contents:** EEG waveforms (all channels) · HRV time series · PPG optical signal · neurofeedback performance scores · session timestamps and duration · protocol parameters used · closed-loop adaptation events · PBM dose (J/cm²) per zone · user-entered symptom/outcome logs · eye-open/closed state during sessions

**SHDR contents:** LED output ratio per zone (**2026-09-27:** per tile, a trend slope only, and only in the idle latest-state record; see the socket-lattice resolution below) · NTC temperature profiles (**2026-09-27:** not per lattice socket during a session, which is UHDR) · EMF shielding attenuation ratio · device session count (unsigned integer, no timestamps) · consumable session counts · USB-C insertion counter · PD negotiation log · impact events (g-force, orientation — between sessions only) · fan RPM · supercapacitor cycles · firmware version history · OTA log · accessory authentication pass/fail · calibration coefficient history

> Neither list is closed, and neither overrides the defining test. A field that is not on a list is
> classified by CLAUDE.md §5.1's defining test, then added — to the boundary-resolution list below
> if the answer needed argument, or to the enumeration above if it did not.

### 5.1 Specific boundary resolutions (list relocated from §5.1)

Specific boundary resolutions:
- Raw EEG impedance → UHDR; derived trend slope → SHDR
- Accelerometer during active sessions → UHDR; impact events between sessions → SHDR **as two derived booleans only** (`drop_detected`, `maintenance_alert` — NP-FW-EMMC-002 §G.2). **Both booleans are computed from unvalidated placeholder thresholds** (`15.0f` g; 3 drop-bearing gaps in 7), so what a row means is not yet established — OI-EMMC2-09. **One bounded exception, NP-FW-EMMC-002 §H:** an *enrolled* device additionally emits a coarsened per-gap impact histogram (counts per fixed g-bin — never a raw series, per-event value, orientation vector or per-event timestamp) into `shdr_accel_characterisation`, for the duration of a time-boxed characterisation window, consented separately by the **warranty owner**, purpose-bound to predictive-maintenance model training only, deleted from the fleet DB at window close. The window is denominated in **records, not calendar time** — this device has no clock that survives a disconnect — and expires fail-closed in firmware. Non-enrolled devices, which is the fleet by default, are governed by §G.3 unqualified. **Caveat, OI-EMMC2-11:** §G.3's ban on a cumulative drop count and on per-drop timing is already defeated by aggregation over `shdr_accel_records`' per-gap rows, independently of §H
- Raw ambient light → UHDR; cumulative UV exposure index → SHDR
- Raw VNS impedance → UHDR; contact resistance trend → SHDR
- Cervical VNS per-electrode impedance reported by the safety MCU to the hub for cross-validation (OI-CVNS-HUB-11) → UHDR (raw tissue impedance), transferred device-internally only, NEVER written to SHDR; the hub-vs-MCU divergence FLAG (`NP_CVNS_SHDR_EV_IMP_CROSSVAL`, no kΩ values, suppressed timestamp) → SHDR **— re-ruled UHDR 2026-11-23** (the principal, `OI-PRIV-40` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004); see the in-session impedance row below). The fail-closed block stays; the SHDR write is removed and code `0xC7` retired, and the fault is logged as a safety-MCU refusal is, so SHDR cannot tell the two apart (`NP-FW-HUB-001` `REQ-FWHUB-92`)
- Cervical VNS re-enable lifecycle after a cardiac cutoff (`np_cvns_reenable.c`, `NP-FW-HUB-001` §7a; `OI-PRIV-28` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004), 2026-11-21). **The cutoff flag** (`NP_CVNS_SHDR_EV_CUTOFF`, `0xC1`) → **UHDR, written nowhere in SHDR; the code is retired and never reused** (the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-22` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004): an interlock that trips on the wearer's heart rate). Until then it went to SHDR under the old "safety interlock log" line. The UHDR session record keeps the cutoff (`cutoff_occurred` and its HR figures); SHDR learns only the cause-free stopped-early bit. **Every other lifecycle event** (the confirmation window opening, a contact check failing or timing out, the re-enable completing, the assertion timing out; formerly SHDR codes `0xC2`–`0xC6`) → **UHDR-class, and written nowhere.** **Why:** they all follow a cutoff, and four happen only after the wearer confirmed resumption, so in sequence, ordered by the session count, they say whether the person resumed neck stimulation after a cardiac event and how many skin-contact attempts it took. That is a fact about the person (the defining test). The pre-session electrode pass/fail that `NP-FW-CVNS-001` §8.2 puts in SHDR is not this: every session has that one, whereas this contact check exists only after a cutoff and the wearer's confirmation.  No UHDR copy is kept: nothing reads one, and the user's app already sees the live re-enable state in `CVNS_FAULT_STATUS` byte 1. Codes `0xC2`–`0xC6` are retired and never reused (`REQ-FWHUB-91`)
- Delivered stimulation current exceeding commanded on BES/tACS or tDCS (`OI-FMEA-09`, `NP-FW-HUB-001` §8.3.1): delivered current itself → UHDR (it stays in the `0x15` stim record); the divergence FLAG (`NP_STIM_SHDR_EV_DELIVERY_DIVERGENCE`, slot and modality only, no current, no magnitude, suppressed timestamp) → SHDR. **Why SHDR:** the channel is a current source, so delivery above its command is a driver or calibration fault whatever the tissue is doing; the flag says which channel misbehaved, not how much, and the modality is already in SHDR's `mods_active_mask`. Magnitude is never uploaded (`NP-FW-NVRAM-001` D-14)
- Commanded stimulation current per channel (`current_ua[]`) and the declared waveform class and half-period, on the hub-to-safety-MCU SPI link (`np_spi_wire_types.h`, `np_safety_spi.c`; `OI-PRIV-41` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004) point 3, 2026-11-21) → **UHDR-class** ("protocol parameters used"), transferred device-internally only and **never written to SHDR**. Earlier code comments called these SHDR as "what the protocol requested"; that reading fails the defining test, because what a person's protocol asked for tells us about the person. The comments now say UHDR-class. The safety MCU uses the values for the charge ceiling and reports none of them; the per-command commanded-dose record (`0x19`) is already UHDR (row below).
- Session-open marker (`0x86` `SESSION_OPEN`, `OI-FMEA-09`): the device session count, written at session start → SHDR. Paired with the session-end record on the same count, a missing END exposes an unclean end (power loss, fault, watchdog). **Why SHDR:** it adds only the count, and an unclean end is less than the `abort_reason` the SHDR session-end record already carries. *(2026-11-21: that comparison no longer holds. `OI-PRIV-21` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004) removed `abort_reason` from the SHDR end record (row below), so the OPEN/END pair is now the only per-session outcome bit in SHDR. The marker is unchanged here; whether it still passes the defining test on its own is noted in `OI-PRIV-21`.)* The commanded-dose record per dispatched command (`0x19`) → UHDR ("protocol parameters used")
- **SHDR session-end record** (`0x80` `SESSION_END`; `OI-PRIV-21` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004 H1), GitHub #949; `NP-FW-HUB-001` §6.1, `REQ-FWHUB-89`; 2026-11-21). **SHDR:** the device session count, the firmware version and (since 2026-11-23) **`stopped_early`**, and nothing else. **`stopped_early`** (the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-22` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004); `REQ-FWHUB-89` (c)) is one byte, 0 or 1, in every record: 1 when the session ended on any abort, when any cervical VNS fault stopped its channel (whatever the reason), or when the safety MCU withdrew a grant the hub still requested (whatever the cause). The cause is never kept: SHDR needs to know that a stop changed the hardware's usage, not why, and a cardiac cutoff, a person's stop, a panic, a watchdog and a hardware fault set the same bit. It is not a conditional redaction: the byte is in every record, and no single cause sets it alone. The fleet schema has no column for it yet (ingest is not built). **UHDR, never SHDR:** the session's duration ("session timestamps and duration"); which modalities ran (`mods_active_mask`: which treatments a person received, "protocol parameters used"); and the abort reason (`np_abort_reason_t` names a photoparoxysmal halt, a user stop and a pause timeout: neurology and behaviour). All three stay in the UHDR end record (`0x11`). **No SHDR record from session telemetry:** the per-tick `0x85` EEG record carried only the count, so its number per session was the duration in seconds; the per-tick zone-slot `0x81` PBM record took the PD ratio, NTC peak, throttle count and fault mask from session data, against F9 below, and its presence said PBM ran. Both are retired; a driver fault still reaches SHDR as a fault record with its time discarded. Decided by the tests: each removed field tells us something about the person, and none moved toward SHDR. **A device-class abort value in SHDR** (for example a reason mapped to none / device fault / log failure) is not recorded: it would be a new SHDR field, which needs a ruling under rule 1 first.
- Cervical VNS session summary (`np_cvns_shdr_summary_t`, `NP-FW-CVNS-001` §4.6, `REQ-CVNS-15`) → **retired; nothing of a cervical VNS session is written to SHDR** (the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-22` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004)). It held three flags after `OI-PRIV-23` (2026-11-21): impedance check passed, cutoff occurred, and the interlock fault reason. Each is, or can carry, an interlock tripped by a reading of the wearer (skin-contact impedance; heart rate, as `HR_CHANGE`), so dropping some would have left the cutoff readable from the rest. All three, and the kΩ values, parameters and duration removed earlier, stay in the UHDR session record (§4.5). The safety MCU fault-log entry of `NP-FW-CVNS-001` §5.6 (`np_cvns_fault_log_entry_t`: session ordinal and reason) is retired with it; it was defined and never written. So is the hub's fault entry in `cvns_fault_cb`, which wrote the reason code itself: every cervical VNS fault now sets the cause-free stopped-early bit instead (`REQ-FWHUB-91`). The impedance-pass flag was also ruled UHDR the same day under `OI-PRIV-40` (the in-session impedance row below), which also retired the hub-versus-safety-MCU disagreement flag (`0xC7`); that fault now leaves exactly what a safety-MCU refusal leaves (`REQ-FWHUB-92`). **Decided by its tests:** `np_mod_cvns_tests` (every fault reason writes no SHDR record and notes one stop), `np_cvns_fai_tests` (`REQ-CVNS-15` (b)), and `scripts/check-redaction-shape.ts`, which lists no cervical VNS SHDR writer
- HD-tDCS (T2) session summary (`np_hd_shdr_summary_t`, `NP-FW-HD-001` §8.2; `OI-PRIV-75` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004), 2026-11-24) → **retired; the session keeps no SHDR record.** Every field it held is UHDR: montage type and anode electrode (the clinical montage and target), anode current and stimulation duration ("protocol parameters used"; "session timestamps and duration"), mean electrode impedance in kΩ (raw tissue impedance), the impedance check's pass flag (the in-session impedance row below) and the abort reason. None described the device's condition, so the struct was removed rather than reshaped. Nothing read it and no SHDR write used it, so no byte reached SHDR. The anode-electrode index had been ruled SHDR in `NP-FW-HD-001` as "device cap wiring position"; that ruling is retired with the struct, because the same document's UHDR table classed the field as the clinical target. **HD-tDCS still contributes to SHDR, as every modality does: through the per-module running totals below** (its port's sessions present and used, with their time, and its faults), never a session-level record
- **Per-module running totals** (`np_module_usage.c`, `NP_CFG_FILE_MODULE_USAGE`; `NP-FW-HUB-001` §6.11, `REQ-FWHUB-93`; `NP-FW-NVRAM-001` §8.5) → **SHDR, as running totals only: ruled by the principal 2026-11-24.** Every modality contributes, per module: sessions present and the time present (exposure to hair, skin oil and sweat is wear, used or not), sessions used and the time used (each use is an on/off and a ramp up and down, and sessions differ in length), and a count per fault code. **The rule that decides it:** use and exposure are known per module so each module's place in its warranty lifetime is known, and they never need a session: totals accumulate on the helmet and the app learns the differences between downloads (the 2026-11-23 Map 3 reasoning, extended from presence to use). **What keeps it SHDR:** one upserted record, no per-session row, no session number, no time stamp, nothing in the SHDR log; the fleet sees the totals only in the upload's snapshot, under its month anchor. A per-session snapshot would let two consecutive snapshots say which modules a person used in that session, which is the montage (UHDR). A tile's row is keyed by its UID (`MODID-1`'s `module_ref` when uploaded); an accessory's by its port, because accessories report no identity (`OI-FWHUB-38`). Dose and throttle totals stay under `NP-MOD-ID-001` §7's opt-in and are not part of this record
- **In-session electrode impedance check, and everything derived from it** (the safety MCU's pre-enable contact check on VNS/HRV, tDCS, BES/tACS and cervical VNS, `np_impedance_check.c`; `NP_SAFETY_STATUS_IMPEDANCE` and its latched slot and transition count; the cervical VNS summary's impedance-pass flag; `NP_CVNS_SHDR_EV_IMP_CROSSVAL`) → **UHDR** (the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-40` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004); brief `docs/human-input/oi-priv-40-impedance-bit-in-fault-latch-status.md`). **Why:** the check measures one circuit that runs through the wearer (skin, gel, hair, placement, anything worn in the path, the leads, the front end), and a driver error reads as a failure too, so its result cannot tell a device fault from a fact about the person; and it exists only in a session on a person. **No SHDR record may disclose it,** by bit, slot or count: masking the bit alone is not enough, because an impedance failure sets only that bit and the slot, so a masked report would read "OK with a slot" (§5.1 rule 2). **The check itself stays:** it is the contact-confirmation interlock (`CLAUDE.md` §4.2) and the tES enable gate; only where its result may go changed. **Device condition comes from a device-only test** through a known load in a test harness or the maintenance dock, with no person in the circuit → SHDR, as all maintenance data is (`NP-FW-HUB-001` D-41). Not built: `OI-FWHUB-36`
- Cervical VNS gel pad contact pass/fail per electrode, with the side of the neck each pad is on (`CVNS_PAD_STATUS`, `OI-ACC-07`) → UHDR (derived from raw tissue impedance; the side reveals the wearer's montage). Delivered to the user's own app for display only — held in memory, never persisted, cleared on disconnect, NEVER written to SHDR
- Cervical VNS offline-fault summary (`CVNS_FAULT_STATUS`, `NP-SW-FAULTMSG-001` P3). Contents: the fault kind and failing-pad side from the active user's own session records, ordered by device session counter (to be replaced by the person's own session order, `OI-PRIV-41` Q1, row "UHDR session identity"), with no timestamps and no heart-rate values → UHDR. Delivered to the user's own app. The app persists only a per-profile "last acknowledged session counter", on the phone. NEVER written to SHDR **Hub store (2026-09-23, §9.6):** the hub persists the last 8 fault stops across all users (session counter, kind, pad side, and the opaque user tag named at the time) and the last-named tag, as one CRC-checked blob in the **UHDR** partition only. A frame never carries another named user's records.
- Safety-MCU cardiac-cutoff persistence (`np_nv_state.c`: which user tags have an outstanding cutoff) → device-internal. Never SHDR-reportable, never timed, like the fault latch's `tick_ms`. **Two bits cross to the app, and only to the app** (`np_safety_nv_report_t`): *the active user's cervical VNS is withheld*, and *someone on this device has an outstanding cutoff*. The second is shown to every profile as the blanket warning. **That is a deliberate, principal-approved disclosure** (2026-09-22): anyone connecting to a shared device learns that *someone* on it had a cardiac cutoff, never who. The alternative let a person switch profiles to get round their own block with no prompt. Its shape is fixed, a flag set whatever the viewer's identity, so it does not also act as an oracle for *which* profile is blocked (§5.1 rule 2)
- UHDR unlock failed-attempt count (`NP-FW-EMMC-002` §C.7, `REQ-EMMC2-01`; "auth lockout state", `NP-FW-NVRAM-001` §3; `OI-PRIV-35`, NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004, 2026-11-21) → **device-internal, neither UHDR nor SHDR content.** A count only: no time stamp, no credential bytes, nothing saying which credential was tried, and reset to zero by every successful unlock. It must be readable before the UHDR partition is unlocked, so it cannot live under the UHDR key; it lives in Config, on the allocation `NP-FW-NVRAM-001` already made. It is never written to SHDR, never uploaded and never in a diagnostic export. Reporting it off the device would be a new field and is decided by §5.1's tests then (a count of wrong guesses on one person's device is evidence that someone tried to read their data, so it starts at UHDR)
- Active-user tag (`ACTIVE_USER`) → **UHDR** (re-ruled 2026-11-21, `OI-PRIV-29` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004), by the defining test). It is now HMAC-SHA-256 of the helmet's serial under the profile's own secret (`REQ-FAULTMSG-01`), so it differs per helmet and cannot be computed from the profile id. A profile made before that change may still be named to a helmet by its old tag, the first four bytes of its random UUID, until the helmet reports nothing outstanding; that old tag is the same on every helmet and joins to the profile id in exported files. Both stay on the phone, the hub's UHDR blob and the safety MCU, and are never uploaded and never written to SHDR. The safety MCU's plaintext copy in flash is `OI-PRIV-25`.
- Profile tag state (`NPIndividualProfile.activeUserTag`: the 32-byte secret, whether the profile predates the per-helmet tag, and the per-helmet tags it has moved to) → **UHDR** (2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-29` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004), by the defining test: the migrated tags say which helmets the person has used). Kept in the profiles value, so it travels with the profile in the device-replacement migration and the UHDR backup, which is what keeps a person the same person to a helmet that holds their cutoff. Assigned by the profile store, never shown, never sent; only the tag derived from it reaches a helmet
- Module UID (tile, and any accessory that carries one) → SHDR-class as a component identifier (`NP-HEX-ZM-001` §4), **but not SHDR-uploadable until `OI-UPG-07` closes** (2026-09-23). Modules carry over from a T1 to its owner's T2 (`NP-REG-UPG-001` Rev 2 §7.0), so a UID seen on two devices joins their SHDR records into *"one owner held both, and moved from a wellness unit to a medical device"*. That is an inference about a person, from a record linked to device and warranty token only. The options are: never upload it, upload a per-device salted form that cannot be joined across devices, or show that the join is harmless. No module UID is uploaded today
- **Socket-lattice PBM telemetry** (principal rulings 2026-09-27, `NP-FW-HUB-001` Rev 16 §6.8–§6.9, fields F1–F14). **The rule that decides every row:** at 80-socket resolution, a per-socket value that exists only for the sockets a session lit reconstructs the treatment montage, and the montage implies the indication (§5.1 rule 2; the fleet schema's DOSE-01). **UHDR:** which sockets were driven (F1) and how many (F13b); dose (F2), measured irradiance (F3) and dose-limit-reached (F4) per socket per wavelength; per-socket NTC now and session peak (F7), which is scalp-contact temperature and marks a lit socket; which socket throttled or was refused as hot (F8); the per-session PD1/PD2 ratio series. **Never stored:** raw PD1/PD2 counts (F5) and the TIA gain used per sample (F6). **SHDR:** **the PD1/PD2 ratio trend slope and the fouling/aging flag (F9, as originally decided in `NP-FW-PBM1064-001` §9.3; the principal briefly ruled it UHDR and reversed that the same day)**, per tile, only in the idle latest-state record and never from session data. *Residual:* rule 1's positive demonstration of no biology content is not on file for the slope, and PD2 is scalp backscatter. Also SHDR: driving faults as modality + code with **no socket** (F11); the tile's latest maintenance verdicts — I²C probe (F11b), calibration source (F12), NTC plausible (the reading itself is discarded), PD1-only LED emission (F10); per-session event counts with no location, written at every session end whether or not PBM ran (F13); occupancy and tile kind (F14), with **no module UID** (see the Module UID row). **The shape of the tile record is itself a ruling:** one latest-state row per (device, socket), written by the idle pass over every occupied socket, with no session count and no run identifier, so SHDR never holds a log of which tiles a person chose to test, or when. **Maintenance self-tests** (§6.9) are run by the warranty owner only, and **all maintenance data is SHDR** (principal, 2026-09-27; `NP-FW-HUB-001` D-41). That passes the defining test because no maintenance output can come from a person: tiles light only with the helmet in a maintenance dock that physically excludes a head and that the hub detects from hardware (`OI-FWHUB-25`); away from the dock a test keeps a verdict and discards the raw reading. "Docked" is read by the hub, never claimed by the app or the user, so this is not a classification by claimed mode (`NP-FW-BENCH-001` D-9). A test that cannot meet those conditions does not belong in the maintenance catalogue
- **Session fitting record** (`SWR-HV-02`, `NP-SW-001` §6.3; principal direction 2026-10-09). **UHDR.** At each session start the helmet writes the helmet hardware version and the module in each wired socket (`NP_LOG_TAG_UHDR_FITTING`, `np_session_fitting.h`): where on a person's head light could be applied is the treatment montage's raw material, and the record is tied to one session. **The shape is a ruling** (§5.1 rule 2): one byte for every wired socket, empty ones included, so the length and layout say nothing about the fitting. It is not the idle occupancy record of F14, which is SHDR, has no session link and carries no socket-to-session relation. The wavelength-rules stamp (origin, release, the three channel windows; fixed length, 39 bytes) is in it, as `NP_FITTING_FORMAT` 2 (`NP-NPPS-REF-001` §7a.1, principal 2026-11-13, GitHub #564; the hub copies it unchanged from the signed descriptor): **UHDR**, because it records how the requested wavelength was applied in that person's session. **The rule definition itself is neither UHDR nor SHDR:** it is owner configuration, held in the warranty-owner profile (`SWR-WO-01`), and says nothing about a person's biology or the device's condition. NeurOne never receives it: it is not in SHDR, a fleet upload or the hub diagnostic log, and any transfer of it is encrypted (`CLAUDE.md` §23).
- **Individual profile in the app** (`np.limits.profiles`; `NP-SW-001` `REQ-SW03-32`; `OI-PRIV-11` part 1, 2026-11-15). **UHDR-class, per field:** `name` → UHDR (on a clinic phone it names a patient). `notes` → UHDR; its purpose is to tell two profiles apart, it is not a record about the person, nothing reads it but the profile list, and it is bounded (`PROFILE_NOTES_MAX_CHARS`). `id` (random UUID) → UHDR while it sits with the name; its first four bytes alone are the active-user tag ruled above. `stealth` (the person's choice to keep the helmet's lights dark, `SWR-IND-01`) → UHDR (the stealth row below). `createdAt` (the time the profile was made; React web and Swift models) → **not kept** (the principal, 2026-11-18): nothing read it, so it is removed as the date of birth was, and a stored profile is rewritten without it. **Date of birth → not kept at all:** no limit or protocol rule on any platform read it (the React web app displayed it and nothing else did; the Swift app declared it and never set it), so it is removed, and a stored profile that carries one is rewritten without it. The profile is held in the app's store, travels only in the person's own backup or migration (`limitsProfiles`, `SWR-MG-01`), and is NEVER written to SHDR, analytics or a research extract. **Why UHDR though it is not health data:** it says who uses this device, and the individual limits keyed by its `id` say what was restricted for that person; when in doubt → UHDR
- **Protocol used flag and the identifiers in an exported file** (`used`, `helmet_id`, `individual_id`, protocol `id` and `family_id`; `OI-PRIV-11` part 2). The used flag says a person ran a protocol → UHDR-class by the defining test; the helmet serial in a limits file is a device identifier and the individual ID is the profile's. **Ruled 2026-11-16 (the principal):** they travel only in the full form, which is the person's own storage, backup and migration. **No screen shows them either (2026-11-18):** the protocol script an editor shows carries no used flag, and a limits script on screen is the share form. A file for someone else is the share form (`NP-SW-001` `SWR-WO-07`): no used flag, no helmet serial, no individual ID, and a new protocol `id` and `family_id`. The name, description, author, tags and conditions are the sender's own words and are written as they are
- **Stealth mode preference** (`STEALTH`, 0x0022; `NP-FW-HUB-001` §5.5a, `REQ-FWHUB-86`; `OI-PRIV-37` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004), 2026-11-21) → **UHDR-class.** That the wearer hides the indicators is a fact about the person: it says they do not want a neuromodulation session seen, and it can mark a particular wearer on a shared device (when in doubt, UHDR). **The hub holds it in RAM only** and every boot starts with it off, so today it is in no record; it travels to and from the app only on an encrypted link (CLAUDE.md §23). NEVER written to SHDR or to Config. **The app keeps it per profile** (`SWR-IND-01`; the principal, 2026-11-23): a field of the individual profile (`stealth`, false unless set; the individual profile row above), held and travelling as the profile does, and sent to the hub at every link. Kept on the hub across a power cycle (a Mode 3 session started without the app) it is not; that would be the UHDR partition under the user's key, open in `OI-PRIV-37`. **The warranty owner's stealth lock** (`SWR-IND-02`, `stealthForbidden` in the owner's per-helmet record) is not about a person and is neither UHDR nor SHDR: owner configuration, kept in the warranty owner's store (`REQ-SHDR-10`) and never in a user's data or an upload. The status-indicator state itself (idle, session, fault) is not recorded anywhere
- IR eye state during sessions → UHDR. **Safety interlocks (narrowed by the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-22` (NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-004)):** an interlock that reports the **device** (a goggle lifted off its Hall sensor, a hot tile throttled, a watchdog, a charge ceiling reached) → SHDR; one that **trips on a reading of the wearer** (the photoparoxysmal halt, the cervical VNS cardiac cutoff, a skin-contact check) → **UHDR**, in every form: no event code, flag, reason or count of it in SHDR. Enforced via the single fixed-shape marshaller `np_fault_latch_build_report()` → `np_fault_latch_report_t` (`{status, count}` since 2026-11-23; `{status, slot, count}` before). **The reported record must never contain a field whose value or presence depends on the latched status word** (2026-08-12): `tick_ms` was previously zeroed only for `NP_SAFETY_STATUS_CARDIAC`, which made `count > 0 && tick_ms == 0` a self-interpreting one-bit cardiac oracle — *worse* than no redaction, because a bare relative SysTick value needs a session record SHDR does not hold, whereas the redaction pattern needed nothing. A redaction conditioned on a sensitive predicate leaks that predicate. Until 2026-11-23 the cardiac predicate was also published to SHDR separately, as `fault_log.fault_type = 'CVNS_HR_CUTOFF'` (`0xC1`); that code is retired (`OI-PRIV-22`).

### 5.2 Predictive maintenance system (SHDR-based)

Three phases:
- **Phase 1** (0–1,000 devices, Year 1): Population-average survival analysis on time-to-failure data
- **Phase 2** (1,000–10,000 devices, Year 2): Fleet-trained LSTM on SHDR sensor trajectories
- **Phase 3** (10,000+ devices, Year 3+): Bayesian personalization — continuously revised RUL predictions

All models version-stamped by hardware revision. New revision falls back to Phase 1 until fleet data accumulates. Models deployed back to devices via OTA — competitive moat grows automatically with fleet size.

**Characterisation cohort (NP-FW-EMMC-002 §H).** Phases 2–3 are the *only* sanctioned consumer of the §H extended impact set. Devices whose warranty owner has opted in contribute a coarsened per-gap impact histogram for the duration of the characterisation window, and receive Phase 2/3 personalisation first — genuine reciprocity rather than an inducement, since a model cannot personalise for a device whose handling it cannot see. Three constraints bind this:

- **Non-coercion (CHAR-4).** Participation buys *earlier and better maintenance prediction*, never baseline safety. Every §4.2 interlock and every safety-critical reminder fires identically for non-participants; this is asserted in `firmware/shdr/tests/np_accel_shdr_tests.c`, not merely stated.
- **Selection bias.** The cohort is self-selected and plausibly differs from the fleet in the variable under study — people who opt into a handling study may handle differently. Per `NP-MOD-ID-001` §7.6, a *negative* result generalises comfortably and a positive one sized on a skewed cohort does not.
- **A known gap in the Phase 2 premise.** Phase 2 is specified as a *"fleet-trained LSTM on SHDR sensor trajectories"*, but roughly thirty SHDR fields are boolean flags derived from thresholds frozen before any fleet existed — flag streams, not trajectories. §H obtains real trajectories for one of those thirty. The general remedy is recorded as **OI-EMMC2-13** and is not adopted.

**Reminder engine rules:**
- Safety-critical: cannot be dismissed — blocks session start
- Performance-critical: snooze max 3×
- Comfort/longevity: snooze max 5×
- All reminders measurement-triggered, not calendar-triggered
- Every reminder includes measured data that triggered it + one-tap order link

### 5.3 Research data anonymization architecture (locked)

All anonymization of UHDR data for research purposes must occur **on-device**, within the NeurOne app, before any data leaves the device. NeurOne cannot access raw UHDR at any point — including for research purposes — because the biometric-derived AES-256 key is never held by NeurOne infrastructure.

**Data flow per approved study:**
1. NeurOne server sends device a signed study descriptor (study ID, approved UHDR element list, anonymization parameters: k≥10 and a date-rounding interval of ≥1 week, and an expiry day after which the device refuses it). Descriptor is cryptographically signed. It carries no suppression rule: that rule is the device's and is the same for every study (the principal, 2026-11-19; CLAUDE.md Rev 82).
1a. **Delivery by subscription (the principal, 2026-11-21, `OI-PRIV-68`; it replaces the 2026-11-20 form that kept a per-person record at the server).** The app does not poll NeurOne; it subscribes. A device subscribes **only while it is the *current* device for at least one of its users**: one subscription per device however many users it holds (a clinic device has one). The subscription is a push channel and says nothing about consent, any user or any study. NeurOne publishes each signed descriptor to every subscribed device and **filters nothing**, by topic, category or consent. On receipt the device verifies the signature, works out the users for whom it is current, and for each runs `ConsentEngine.admit()` against that user's consents, on the device only; a user whose consents do not admit it is refused and nothing is returned. **A request is published once and is never re-published when the current device changes:** a device that became current just after a publish misses it and receives the next, and the data on the two devices is the same data, so nothing is missed (the principal, 2026-11-20).

**Current is per user and set by the person, never by the server.** Each person has a *current-device key*: an Ed25519 seed made on the device, held in the UHDR and carried by a migration or restore, never rotated; its *key id* is the SHA-256 of the public key. A migration or restore asks the research consents again (grants are never carried) and **is not finished until they are answered**; it then makes the new device current for that person, subscribes the device if it is not subscribed, and publishes a signed **superseded notice**: the key id and a count that rises with each move, signed with the key. The server stores the notice and broadcasts it to all subscribed devices without reading it. A device that holds that key verifies the signature against the public key it has and, if the count is higher than the one it knows, stops being current for that person; a device current for no one unsubscribes. A notice that does not verify is ignored. **Catch-up (the principal, 2026-11-21):** the server keeps only the highest-count notice per key id and never expires one, and gives each notice a sequence number that names nothing; every publish carries the latest sequence; a device whose cursor is behind it fetches the notices after its cursor before it extracts anything. **The last device registered is the current one**: a person with several devices must be told that research follows the last (strings in `locales/<bcp47>.json`, `CLAUDE.md` §17; not written, no registration screen exists). A device set up from scratch holds no key, so it is a new person to the system with a key of its own and is no takeover. The notice and the subscription are sent when the restore is finished and not before, and a restore does not wait for the server: until they are sent, the previous device stays current, and the person is told. A lost device that is never retired is a known edge (the principal, 2026-11-21): its requests go to a device nobody can use, and a push service that reports its channel invalid lets NeurOne drop it. The key and its signature are cryptographic code, so they are written once in `common/npps-core` (`CLAUDE.md` §22).

**What the push costs (accepted by the principal, 2026-11-21).** The platform's push service learns that the app receives these broadcasts. NeurOne holds a stable channel address for each subscribed device; it names no user and is kept apart from the research store, the SHDR fleet database and the warranty database. A device on a platform with no push service, or with push refused, cannot be current for research. **A web push cannot be silent, so every publish shows the person a neutral notice, the same fixed text for every publish and naming no study; the principal accepted it, even welcomed it (2026-11-22).** A Windows push channel expires after 30 days and cannot be renewed, so the app requests a new one and sends `subscribe` again (through the relay) when it starts and, for a person who does not open it, from a background task; macOS delivery to a background process is not yet shown to work (`OI-PRIV-68`). **The push carries only the latest request and notice sequence numbers, never the request** (the principal, 2026-11-21): it wakes the app, and the app fetches the request through the Oblivious HTTP relay (`OI-PRIV-69`), so the push service learns that something was published and not what. The app does not fetch on opening, and a push that is dropped means the request is missed. Nothing is sent back to NeurOne for further anonymization: anonymization is complete on the device before anything is transmitted (steps 2 and 3).
2. App reads encrypted UHDR partition in-app, applies on-device anonymization transformations: k-anonymity grouping, date/time rounding, direct identifier removal, and suppression by the device's fixed rule: a record whose group is smaller than k is dropped, not sent (`REQ-CONSENT-13` e).
3. Only the pre-anonymized extract, carrying an anonymous token that shows it came from a genuine device without naming it (the principal, 2026-11-23, `OI-PRIV-74`; `NP-FW-ANON-001` §7.3), is transmitted to NeurOne research infrastructure. Raw UHDR never leaves the device. **Through the Oblivious HTTP relay and gateway of `NP-INFRA-001` §3.3b, so NeurOne never sees the device's network address, and in several messages when it is larger than one message holds** (the principal, 2026-11-21, `OI-PRIV-69`); every device-to-NeurOne research call (`subscribe`, `unsubscribe`, `supersede`, the notice fetch and the extract upload) takes the same path and none ships before the relay does.
4. NeurOne servers store extract keyed to **study ID only**: no device identifier, token or serial travels with an extract (the principal, 2026-11-20, `OI-PRIV-67`). No persistent per-user anonymized data store. No linkage table exists, and nothing in the store joins to SHDR, the warranty database or a person. Duplicate loading of one person's UHDR from several devices is prevented on the devices, not by a device key in the store: a device extracts for a person only while it is current for them, and a move retires the old device by a signed notice it can verify (§5.3 step 1a; the principal, 2026-11-21, `OI-PRIV-68`, not built). **Tracking over time (the principal, 2026-11-20, `OI-PRIV-70`):** a study declares in its signed descriptor, up front and for good, whether it needs it; the default is none. **None:** the extract carries no identifier, a person's extract for the study is made once, and the study cannot ask a second time (a second request could not avoid duplicates from anyone); the device keeps the study's participation record so it will not extract twice. **Declared:** the device derives an identifier unique to that person and that study from a UHDR-held secret and the study ID (not the current-device key, and never the same across studies), sends it with each release, and keeps a marker so a release carries only data after the last sent (dates are rounded, so the marker falls on a rounded-period boundary and a period is sent only when complete). The invitation tells the person, computed from the descriptor, that the researchers can link their releases; consent to it is per project and carries the irreversibility notice; k-anonymity applies to the linked series and not to each release alone. A study that began without tracking cannot add it; that is a new study with fresh consent. Open for the IRB adviser: what a researcher must show to declare tracking, and the exemption argument of `NP-IRB-001` §5 for a coded identifier.
5. Researchers access aggregated study datasets with no device ID fields.

**Consent withdrawal effect:** Because each study extract is generated on-device on-demand, withdrawing consent permanently blocks the device from processing future study descriptors. No further extracts are generated or transmitted — **for any data period, including sessions predating withdrawal**. Already-published extracts cannot be individually removed from datasets (irreversibility notice given at consent time); no new data flows ever.

**Audit trail (UHDR; the principal, 2026-11-20, `OI-PRIV-07`):** Study ID, study descriptor hash, enrolment day, extract transmission day, extract byte count and withdrawal day are logged in the UHDR on the device, under the person's key. The person can inspect every study they contributed to in the app. This log is never shared with researchers and never uploaded to SHDR. SHDR holds nothing that names a study and no running total of extracts or bytes. The record is UHDR because a study ID resolves to a disease-category study and a withdrawal day is a personal decision, and a device-linked token would tell the fleet database which wearer is in which study (§5.1 defining test; rule 1). SHDR upload is consented by the warranty owner, who may be a clinic and is not the wearer (CLAUDE.md §6.0), and a shared device would mix wearers in one log. NeurOne's own record of an extract is the research store's receipt (step 4), keyed to study ID only, so NeurOne holds per-study counts and no per-device account (`OI-PRIV-67`).
