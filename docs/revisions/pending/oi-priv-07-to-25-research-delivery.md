## CLAUDE.md
text: **Rev {CLAUDE.md to} ({date}) — §5.3, §6.3: the study audit trail is UHDR, the research store is keyed to study ID only, a study declares up front whether it needs tracking over time, and research requests are published to subscribed devices, a device subscribing only while it is the current device for one of its users (the principal, `OI-PRIV-07`, `OI-PRIV-21`, `OI-PRIV-22`, `OI-PRIV-24`). No other locked decision changed.**
text: **What changed.** §5.3's last sentence said study ID, descriptor hash, transmission timestamp and byte count are logged in SHDR; it now says the audit trail, with the enrolment and withdrawal days, is UHDR on the device and never uploaded, and that no device identifier reaches the research store. It gains the tracking declaration: a study states up front in its signed descriptor whether it needs tracking, the default is none, and a study that declares it sends an identifier unique to the person and the study, with plain-words consent and k-anonymity on the linked series. §6.3 no longer says NeurOne builds an invitation list from device ID and contact preferences. A request is published to every subscribed device and nothing at the server is filtered by topic, category or consent. A device subscribes only while it is the current device for at least one of its users, one subscription per device, and the subscription says nothing about consent, any user or any study. The device compares each user's consents with the request on the device. A person is current on the last device they registered, and a move publishes a signed notice that retires the old one. Delivery is a platform push, which the push service can see, accepted.
text: **Why.** §5.3 conflicted with the §5.1 defining test: a study ID resolves to a disease-category study and a withdrawal day is a personal decision, and under the device-linked SHDR token the log would tell the fleet database which wearer is in which study. Studies do not need device data, and the one use found for it, stopping one person's UHDR being loaded from several devices, is met by delivering only to the current device. Researchers sometimes need to follow a participant across tranches, so a study may declare tracking, but only up front and for good. An earlier form of §6.3 kept a per-person record at the server and had the server learn who consented to some research, which is the leak `NP-SW-PORTAL-API-001` §3 existed to prevent, so everything about the person moved onto the device. The principal's rulings, 2026-11-20 to 2026-11-22.
text: **What it leaves.** Nothing is built beyond the superseded notice in the core (`SWR-MG-34`): no subscribe, publish, fetch or restore consent step exists, and the study service's side is `OI-CONSENT-07`. The IRB adviser's answer on what a researcher must show to declare tracking, and on the `NP-IRB-001` §5 exemption for a coded identifier, is open (`docs/human-input/oi-priv-24-tracking-declaration.md`, not sent). Delivery to a non-running macOS app is not shown to work, and the push limits for APNs and FCM are unconfirmed (`OI-PRIV-22`). The §1 to §6 and §16 to §23 numbering is unchanged.
