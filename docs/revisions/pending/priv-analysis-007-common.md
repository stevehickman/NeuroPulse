That is deliberate and is the useful half of this item: it makes "wire up a transport and skip the signature check" unrepresentable rather than merely discouraged, so closing this needs the key distribution decided, not assumed. **Outbound:** §6.3 step 4's *Ask a question* ("secure message to a NeurOne liaison, 2 business day response") has nowhere to send the question — `askQuestionAboutInvitation()` records that one was asked and leaves the invitation open, and both UIs tell the user to contact NeurOne rather than implying a message was delivered, which is honest and is not the workflow §6.3 describes. **Not urgent and not a defect:** the research portal these serve is not built either, and every decision path is exercisable from the dashboard. So this is a key-management item, not a coding one, and writing an HTTP client would not have touched it. **What landed (§4a):** `StudyDescriptorCanonicalForm` on both platforms — the one schema decision the spec takes, because *"descriptors are cryptographically signed"* is not implementable until something says what is signed — and `Ed25519StudyDescriptorVerifier` over it. The form is **length-prefixed rather than delimited** (`studyTitle` is server-supplied text and can contain the separator; a delimiter-joined encoding lets a crafted title shift field boundaries so that one signature covers two descriptors — both suites carry that forgery attempt), **sorts sets and preserves list order**, and **names categories and elements by a stable wire spelling** rather than by whichever serializer each platform happens to use: iOS `rawValue`, and on Android an explicit `wireName` with `displayName` now derived from it, so localization reaching `:core` later cannot move the wire. **One finding came out of building it:** iOS modelled the issue date as an instant and Android as a day, so the canonical form must use the coarser — **time of day is not covered by the signature**, and an issuer must not rely on it to distinguish two descriptors. The day renders in UTC on a fixed Gregorian calendar so a descriptor cannot verify in one time zone and fail in another. Ed25519, and the **raw 32 bytes** as the key on both platforms even though the JDK wants SPKI, so "the NeurOne study-signing key" is one artifact rather than two. `RefusingStudyDescriptorVerifier` remains the `ConsentStore` default, and an absent, malformed or wrong-length key yields **no verifier at all** rather than one that throws at the first descriptor — a misconfigured build falls back to refusing. **Still open, and what closing needs:** who holds the study-review signing key, how it is rotated, and how it reaches an app build. No endpoint or schema beyond the signing form is specified, on purpose (§7).
