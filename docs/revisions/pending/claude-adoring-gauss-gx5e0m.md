text: - [ ] **`OI-PRIV-79` (raised 2026-11-25, `NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-006` F-14, checked; `CLAUDE.md` §5.1; NP-SW-001 Rev 89, `REQ-SW03-18` (b); `app/web/src/lib/protocolBackup.ts`, `app/web/src/lib/limitsStore.ts` `exportLimitsAsNPPS`; `common/lib/bundleKey.ts`; brief `docs/human-input/oi-priv-79-web-backup-file-sealing.md`; GitHub #1068) — should the React web app's backup file and its individual-limits download be sealed?** The principal chose memory-only with a backup file for the React app (NP-SW-001 Rev 89): nothing is kept in the browser, and *Save my protocols* writes the person's protocols to a `.npps` file so a reload does not lose them. That file, and the download of a person's individual limits, are plain text, readable by anything that can read the person's downloads folder or the place they keep it. Whether that is an unsealed copy of UHDR under `CLAUDE.md` §5.1 was not weighed when the design was chosen. The other apps' backups are sealed under a recovery code or passphrase the person holds (`SWR-MG-02`), and the web already has that path (`common/lib/bundleKey.ts`, the shared core's Argon2id key). **Not changed by the repair of F-14**, because the design was the principal's. **Options (principal):** (A) seal both under a code or passphrase through the bundle path; (B) seal the backup only, and leave the limits download plain because it is meant to be read or handed to a clinician; (C) keep both plain, and say on the save button that the file is not protected. **Closes when:** the principal has chosen, and the files and `REQ-SW03-18` (b) follow it.
text: - [ ] **`OI-PRIV-80` (raised 2026-11-25, from repairing `NP-PRIV-ANALYSIS-006` F-16; `REQ-SW03-37`; BIPA 740 ILCS 14/15(a); `CLAUDE.md` §5.1; `app/NeurOneUI/shared/src/commonMain/kotlin/life/neurone/shared/ui/SettingsScreen.kt`, `EegRevokedDialog.kt`, `session/RecordingDeletion.kt`) — must withdrawing the biometric (EEG) release destroy the recordings already made?** The written release says recordings stay on the person's device until they delete them, and refers to a published retention and destruction policy. The repair (`REQ-SW03-37`) tells the person, at the withdrawal, that recordings already made stay, on this device and in any backup, and offers to delete the ones on this device; it deletes nothing by itself. Three things it does not decide. (1) Whether a withdrawal must destroy the recordings without being asked, or within a set time: BIPA 14/15(a) requires destruction when the initial purpose has been satisfied, and a withdrawal may be read as ending it. (2) The copies in a backup: the app cannot reach a backup that is a file the person saved, and an automatic backup keeps a recording after the device's copy is deleted (`SWR-MG-03`, `REQ-SW03-29`), so whether a withdrawal should also stop backing recordings up, or remove them from the backups the app can reach. (3) A copy on the helmet: whether the hub keeps a recording after the app has downloaded it was not checked. **Options (principal, with a BIPA legal view):** (a) keep the person's choice as built, and make the published policy say so; (b) delete the device's recordings at the withdrawal, after saying so, and remove them from the backups the app can reach; (c) as (b), and have the hub erase its copies. **Closes when:** the principal has chosen, the published policy and the release say what the app does, and `REQ-SW03-37` follows it.
