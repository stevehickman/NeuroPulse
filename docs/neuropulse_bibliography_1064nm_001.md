### Entry 12.1 — Yao et al. (2022) | PRIMARY EVIDENCE

**Citation:** Yao Y, Huang C, Li Z, Yin H, Huo C, Zhou D, Li X, Wang Y, Tian F, Liu H, Luo Q. "Transcranial photobiomodulation with 1064 nm laser modulates brain electroencephalogram rhythms." *Science Advances* 8, eabj7390 (2022). DOI: [10.1126/sciadv.abj7390](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj7390)  
**Institution:** University of Texas at Dallas (Hanli Liu lab) + Capital Medical University Beijing + other Chinese institutions  
**Design:** Randomised sham-controlled crossover, n = 20 healthy adults, double-blinded for sham condition  
**Modality:** 1064 nm CW laser applied transcranially to right prefrontal cortex (F4 position); ~250 mW/cm² irradiance; 8-minute session

**What this study shows:**
- 1064nm transcranial laser applied to right prefrontal cortex significantly improved visual working memory capacity in healthy adults (p < 0.01)
- Primary EEG measure: contralateral delay activity (CDA) amplitude — an established electrophysiological index of visual working memory storage capacity. CDA amplitude increased significantly vs sham, indicating greater neural resources engaged for working memory maintenance.
- Secondary EEG finding: modulation of gamma-band (30–50 Hz) and alpha-band (8–12 Hz) rhythms at right prefrontal electrodes during the PBM session window
- fNIRS monitoring confirmed increased cerebral oxygenation (HbO₂) at the stimulation site, validating that light reached cortical tissue and produced a vascular response
- Effects were spatially specific: responses observed primarily at right frontal and parietal electrodes, consistent with the right PFC stimulation site and the known lateralisation of visual working memory processes

**What this study does not show:**
- Study used a laser (coherent, collimated beam) not an LED array. NeuroPulse uses 150 × 1064nm LED emitters per zone module (EPITEX L1064-02AU, Lambertian emission pattern). The photobiomodulation literature supports equivalent biological effect between coherent (laser) and non-coherent (LED) sources at equivalent irradiance and dose, but this specific study cannot be directly cited as LED evidence.
- Does not demonstrate effects in clinical populations (depression, MCI, TBI) — this is a healthy adult study. Clinical translation requires separate studies.
- Working memory improvement duration was assessed immediately post-session and at 1-hour follow-up; long-term or cumulative effects across multiple sessions were not assessed.
- Does not demonstrate effects at NeuroPulse's pulsed operating mode (NeuroPulse uses PWM at 2–40 Hz; this study used CW). Effects of pulsed 1064nm on working memory have not been independently confirmed in a registered trial.
- Single stimulation site (right F4). NeuroPulse applies stimulation across 5 zones spanning frontal, parietal, and occipital positions; the spatial generalisation of right prefrontal findings to other zones is not established.

**NeuroPulse application:**
- Primary evidence basis for the 1064nm working memory protocol (Gamma Clarity and Focus Prime presets, NP-SES-1064-001 Rev A §2).
- Supports the claim that 1064nm reaches cortical tissue and produces measurable electrophysiological and haemodynamic responses — directly addresses the depth-tier penetration claim's functional plausibility.
- The EEG CDA finding provides the mechanistic rationale for EEG-adaptive operation: if 1064nm modulates gamma and alpha rhythms, then EEG-adaptive frequency (closed-loop band targeting) is a biologically relevant parameter for 1064nm, not only for the BES/tACS and Audio Entrainment modalities.
- **FTC substantiation role:** This study constitutes the primary clinical evidence substantiating the 1064nm cognition claim for the Gamma Clarity and Focus Prime presets. Together with penetration depth evidence (Entry 12.3) it partially substantiates the depth-tier claim. Regulatory counsel assessment of adequacy required (NP-REG-PBM1064-001 Rev A Q10–Q11).

### Entry 12.3 — Tissue Optics and Penetration Depth Basis

**Claim supported:** "Deeper than 810nm: NeuroPulse's 1064nm channel reaches cortical tissue layers at depths not accessible to 810nm devices"

**Biophysical basis:**

Tissue penetration depth in biological tissue (scalp + skull + brain) is governed by the effective attenuation coefficient µ_eff = √(3µ_a(µ_a + µ_s')), where µ_a is absorption coefficient and µ_s' is reduced scattering coefficient. The penetration depth δ = 1/µ_eff.

| Wavelength | Primary absorbers | µ_a (brain) approx. | Effective penetration depth | Source |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|----------------------------|--------|
| 660 nm | Oxyhemoglobin, melanin | ~0.30 cm⁻¹ | ~8–12 mm | Torricelli et al. (2014); Strangman et al. (2002) |
| 810 nm | Oxyhemoglobin trough | ~0.05–0.10 cm⁻¹ | ~20–25 mm | Torricelli et al. (2014) |
| 1064 nm | Low absorption "window" | ~0.05–0.08 cm⁻¹ | ~28–35 mm | Bashkatov et al. (2005); Yaroslavsky et al. (2002) |
| 1170 nm | Between water peaks (970/1450nm) | ~0.10–0.15 cm⁻¹ | ~35–40 mm | Smith et al. (2009) T2 |

**Key references for penetration depth:**

**Bashkatov AN, Genina EA, Kochubey VI, Tuchin VV.** "Optical properties of human skin, subcutaneous and mucous tissues in the wavelength range from 400 to 2000 nm." *J Phys D: Appl Phys* 38, 2543–2555 (2005). DOI: [10.1088/0022-3727/38/15/004](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3727/38/15/004)  
*Comprehensive tissue optics data for skin and subcutaneous tissue across 400–2000nm; includes absorption and reduced scattering spectra. The 1064nm region shows reduced absorption vs the 808nm water-hemoglobin window, supporting deeper penetration claims.*

**Yaroslavsky AN, Schulze PC, Yaroslavsky IV, Schober R, Ulrich F, Schwarzmaier H-J.** "Optical properties of selected native and coagulated human brain tissues in vitro in the visible and near infrared spectral range." *Phys Med Biol* 47, 2059–2073 (2002). DOI: [10.1088/0031-9155/47/12/305](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/47/12/305)  
*Measured optical properties of human brain tissue (grey matter, white matter) in vitro from 400–1800nm. The 1064nm data point shows the lowest µ_a in the measurement range for cortical grey matter — directly supports 1064nm as optimal for cortical depth penetration. White matter at 1064nm also shows very low absorption. This is foundational tissue optics data for the depth-tier claim.*

**Strangman G, Boas DA, Sutton JP.** "Non-invasive neuroimaging using near-infrared light." *Biol Psychiatry* 52, 679–693 (2002). DOI: [10.1016/s0006-3223(02)01550-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-3223(02)01550-0)  
*Reviews fNIRS methodology and optical window in neural tissue. Provides wavelength comparison data supporting the differential penetration of NIR wavelengths through scalp, skull, and cortex. Cited in fNIRS and transcranial PBM literature as the canonical wavelength comparison reference.*

**What these studies show:** Biological tissue has lowest absorption (and therefore greatest depth penetration) for NIR light in the range 900–1100nm. At 1064nm, both scalp tissue and brain tissue show lower absorption coefficients than at 808nm, resulting in greater effective penetration depth (approximately +30–40% greater penetration depth at 1064nm vs 808nm based on published µ_a values).

**What these studies do not show:** In vitro tissue optics measurements do not directly translate to in vivo therapeutic depth in living subjects. Factors including tissue blood volume, vessel diameter distribution, skull thickness, and individual anatomical variation all affect in vivo penetration. The tissue optics data provides a biophysical basis for the claim but does not constitute a clinical demonstration that 1064nm reaches deeper cortical structures in therapeutic use. The functional evidence (Yao et al. 2022) provides the in vivo confirmation that 1064nm produces cortical effects; neither type of evidence alone is sufficient for the full depth claim.

**NeuroPulse application:** Together with Yao et al. (2022), the tissue optics literature provides two-pronged substantiation for the depth-tier penetration claim: (1) biophysical mechanism (lower tissue absorption at 1064nm → greater penetration depth), and (2) functional human evidence (1064nm produces cortical EEG and haemodynamic responses confirming cortical reach). This combination constitutes partial substantiation per FTC guidelines; regulatory counsel assessment of adequacy required (NP-REG-PBM1064-001 Rev A Q10).

### Entry 12.4 — Mechanism: Cytochrome c Oxidase at 1064nm

**Citation:** Rojas JC, Gonzalez-Lima F. "Neurological and psychological applications of transcranial lasers and LEDs." *Biochem Pharmacol* 86, 447–457 (2013). DOI: [10.1016/j.bcp.2013.06.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bcp.2013.06.012)  
**Institution:** University of Texas at Austin  
**Design:** Review article  

**What this study shows:**
- Comprehensive review of the cytochrome c oxidase (CCO) chromophore mechanism for NIR PBM in the CNS. CCO (mitochondrial Complex IV, the terminal electron acceptor in oxidative phosphorylation) has absorption bands throughout the NIR, including at approximately 665nm, 810–830nm, and broad absorption extending through 900–1100nm.
- Reviews evidence for CCO as the primary photoacceptor for therapeutic NIR light in neurons: PBM increases CCO enzymatic activity, ATP production, and metabolic rate in neurons.
- Relevant to 1064nm: the CCO absorption spectrum at 1064nm lies in the Soret-equivalent NIR peak and overlaps the "resting-state" (oxidised) CCO absorption, providing a biologically plausible mechanism for 1064nm therapeutic effects via the same CCO pathway as 808nm.
- Reviews human and animal studies of transcranial laser effects on cognition and brain function; provides the mechanistic grounding for the Yao et al. (2022) findings.

**What this study does not show:**
- Does not report original 1064nm-specific CCO absorption or action spectrum data; the review draws on established CCO spectroscopy.
- Does not demonstrate that CCO activation at 1064nm is equivalent in magnitude or duration to CCO activation at 808nm in human subjects.
- Does not address LED vs laser comparison at 1064nm.

**NeuroPulse application:** Provides mechanistic justification for extending the CCO-based PBM mechanism (already established for NeuroPulse's 660nm and 808nm channels) to the 1064nm channel. The shared CCO mechanism supports marketing language about "targeting cellular energy metabolism at three penetration depths." Confirms that the 1064nm channel operates via the same biologically validated pathway as the existing modalities, not a novel uncharacterised mechanism.

### Entry 12.5 — 1064nm Safety Literature

**Reference class:** LED arrays at 1064nm, applied to scalp transcranially, have not been independently reported as causing adverse effects in the published literature. The relevant safety considerations are:

1. **Thermal:** 1064nm has lower water absorption than wavelengths near 1450nm or 970nm, reducing thermal risk vs those wavelengths. All published transcranial PBM safety literature (reviewed in Hamblin 2016 and Naeser et al. 2016) reports absence of significant adverse effects at irradiances up to 250 mW/cm² CW for scalp PBM. NeuroPulse's pulsed operation (25% duty cycle; 100 mW/cm² average) is at or below these values.

2. **Retinal hazard:** 1064nm is in the Nd:YAG laser wavelength class and is known to be focusable by the lens onto the retina (unlike wavelengths > 1400nm which are absorbed in the anterior eye). The NeuroPulse zone modules are scalp-applied; the goggle system (Hall sensor + IR proximity, hardware cutoff) prevents any 1064nm irradiance from reaching the goggle aperture during normal operation. IEC 62471 EH2 assessment is required (see NP-REG-PBM1064-001 Rev A §3.2).

3. **IEC 62471 review:** The IEC 62471 assessment for 1064nm at NeuroPulse parameters is an open item (NP-REG-PBM1064-001 Rev A Q1–Q2). No adverse event data in the published literature contradicts safe use at these parameters, but formal regulatory assessment is required before marketing claims.

**Key safety reference:** Hamblin MR. "Shining light on the head: Photobiomodulation for brain disorders." *BBA Clin* 6, 113–124 (2016). DOI: [10.1016/j.bbacli.2016.09.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbacli.2016.09.002). Reviews human transcranial PBM studies across multiple wavelengths; no reports of serious adverse events across reviewed studies; thermal and retinal considerations reviewed.

### Entry 12.6 — Multi-Modal Combination Studies (1064nm + Other Modalities)

**Status:** No published RCT combines 1064nm PBM with EEG neurofeedback, HRV biofeedback, BES/tACS, or VNS as of the date of this document (2026-05-13). The multi-modal combination evidence gap noted in CLAUDE.md §12 ("No RCT of combined multi-modal protocol exists anywhere") applies to 1064nm PBM as well.

The closest multi-modal combination evidence is:

**Yao et al. (2022)** (Entry 12.1): Used simultaneous fNIRS monitoring with EEG during 1064nm PBM. While fNIRS is monitoring not intervention, this demonstrates the technical feasibility of simultaneous multi-modal physiological measurement during 1064nm sessions.

**The 2025 multi-modal RCT** (referenced in CLAUDE.md §3, HRV biofeedback): This RCT combines PBM + qEEG neurofeedback + HRV biofeedback. Wavelength used: 810nm (not 1064nm). The multi-modal interaction evidence does not directly apply to 1064nm.

**NeuroPulse position:** The NeuroPulse 1064nm + EEG-adaptive closed-loop combination is a novel protocol with no direct RCT evidence as of this writing. Marketing claims for the combined protocol must be framed as technology performance claims ("EEG-adaptive frequency matching during 1064nm session") rather than clinical outcomes claims ("improves cognition better than 1064nm alone"). No compound efficacy claim (1064nm + EEG adaptive > 1064nm alone) is substantiated by current literature. This restriction applies to all app copy, product page descriptions, and investor materials.
