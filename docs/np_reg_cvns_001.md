## 1. Purpose

This document constitutes the Pre-Submission (Q-Sub) package for the NeurOne Pro (T2) cervical transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation (tcVNS) accessory. It presents the proposed 510(k) regulatory pathway, substantial equivalence arguments, and key questions for FDA review.

Q-Sub meeting target: approximately Month 20 from T1 launch (free, prevents avoidable filing grounds). A separate 510(k) submission is required for the cervical tcVNS accessory; the T1 auricular VNS (no carotid proximity) operates under the general wellness pathway and is not within scope of this document.

## 2. Device Description

### 2.1 Device name (proposed)

NeurOne Pro Cervical VNS Accessory (working name; subject to trademark clearance per docs/status/pending-decisions.md §13.1)

### 2.2 Device classification

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Device class | Class II |
| Product code | GZF (non-invasive vagus nerve stimulator) |
| Regulation | 21 CFR 882.5800 |
| Pathway | 510(k) with substantial equivalence to electroCore gammaCore |

### 2.3 Intended use

The NeurOne Pro Cervical VNS Accessory is a transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulator intended for non-invasive transcutaneous stimulation of the cervical vagus nerve via skin-surface electrodes placed over the carotid sheath of the neck. Proposed indications (consistent with predicate cleared indications):

| Indication | Predicate support |
|-----------|------------------|
| Acute treatment of episodic cluster headache | K163334 (electroCore gammaCore, 2016) |
| Adjunctive treatment of episodic cluster headache in adults | K173323 (electroCore gammaCore, 2017) |
| Acute treatment of migraine without aura | K173323 |

**Q-Sub Question 1:** Does FDA agree that the proposed intended use statement adequately mirrors the cleared indications for electroCore gammaCore (K163334 and K173323), and is the 510(k) pathway appropriate for this device and proposed indications?

### 2.4 Device description

The NeurOne Pro Cervical VNS Accessory consists of:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Neck module housing | Shore 30A silicone over-moulded unit; bilateral gel electrode assembly |
| Gel electrodes | Two self-adhesive hydrogel pads applied to skin overlying the carotid sheath |
| Connecting cable | 4-conductor shielded cable to NeurOne hub accessory port |
| Hub integration | Stimulation current generated by NeurOne Pro (T2) hub; safety MCU owns enable GPIO |
| Consumable | Gel pad 5-pack; single-use per electrode contact surface |

**Stimulation waveform:** Biphasic charge-balanced; cathodic-first; configurable pulse width 200–1000 µs; frequency 1–25 Hz; current 0–2 mA; session duration 60–120 seconds.

**Key safety differentiator from predicate:** Integrated cardiac rhythm monitor interlock — safety MCU independently monitors R-peak signal and enforces automatic stimulation cutoff within 100 ms if HR changes > 15 BPM within 5 seconds of stimulation onset. This is an additional safety feature not present in the gammaCore handheld device; it does not change the substantial equivalence analysis but is a safety enhancement.

**Key differences and mitigations:**

| Difference from predicate | Mitigation / analysis |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Lower maximum current (2 mA vs 24 mA) | NeurOne operates at ≤ 2 mA — a **more conservative** operating range than the cleared predicate. The 24 mA predicate upper limit encompasses the NeurOne 2 mA range. This difference does not raise new safety questions. |
| Hub-connected (not standalone handheld) | Different form factor but equivalent function: both deliver transcutaneous electrical stimulation to the cervical vagus nerve. Safety MCU architecture provides equal or greater independence from host software compared to the gammaCore standalone processor. The 510(k) cleared intended use is device-function-based, not form-factor-specific. |
| Cardiac interlock added | Additional safety feature. Does not introduce new risks; reduces existing identified risk (baroreceptor reflex HR change). FDA precedent: additional safety monitoring in 510(k) devices is generally viewed favourably and does not break substantial equivalence. |
| Variable frequency 1–25 Hz | gammaCore uses fixed 25 Hz. NeurOne range includes 25 Hz and extends to lower frequencies. At 25 Hz the devices are equivalent; the additional lower frequency range (1–24 Hz) is within the same stimulation modality. Per FDA guidance, expanded parameter range within the same intended use does not break substantial equivalence if the additional parameters do not create new risks. The lower frequencies (1–24 Hz) result in less vagal activation, not more — a conservative extension. |
| Configurable pulse width 200–1000 µs | gammaCore fixed at 1 ms. NeurOne range includes 1000 µs and extends to shorter widths. Shorter pulse widths at the same current result in less charge per phase — more conservative. 1000 µs upper limit matches predicate exactly. |
| Bilateral stimulation option | gammaCore: unilateral. Bilateral application of transcutaneous VNS is within the same stimulation modality. Clinical literature supports bilateral cervical VNS without evidence of increased adverse events at the NeurOne current range (≤ 2 mA). Q-Sub Question 2 below addresses this. |

### 3.3 Substantial equivalence argument

**Proposed SE finding:** The NeurOne Pro Cervical VNS Accessory has the **same intended use** as the gammaCore predicates (K163334, K173323) — transcutaneous non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation for the treatment of cluster headache and migraine — and uses the **same or safer technological characteristics**. The differences identified do not raise new questions of safety and effectiveness.

**Key SE criteria:**

1. **Same intended use:** Acute treatment of episodic cluster headache and migraine, matching the cleared indications for K163334 and K173323.

2. **Same type of device:** Non-invasive transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulator applied to the cervical vagus nerve via skin-surface electrodes.

3. **Same technology:** Biphasic charge-balanced electrical stimulation. The waveform topology is identical to the predicate; the difference is a more conservative current range.

4. **No new safety questions from technological differences:** The lower maximum current, additional cardiac interlock, and variable frequency range within the predicate modality do not introduce new safety risks. Each difference is either equivalent to or more conservative than the cleared predicate.

## 4. Q-Sub Questions for FDA

### Q1 — Intended use and pathway

Does FDA agree that the proposed intended use statement (§2.3) accurately mirrors the cleared indications for electroCore gammaCore (K163334 and K173323), and that the 510(k) pathway with SE to both predicates is appropriate for the proposed device and indications?

### Q2 — Bilateral stimulation

The gammaCore predicates cleared unilateral cervical stimulation. NeurOne proposes to offer bilateral stimulation as an option. Does FDA require a separate clinical study for the bilateral configuration, or is the bilateral use supported by the predicate's established safety and effectiveness at lower current levels (≤ 2 mA total per electrode pair)?

*NeurOne position:* At 2 mA maximum per electrode, bilateral activation is within the charge density and current ranges demonstrated safe in the gammaCore clinical programme. The bilateral configuration is a user convenience option, not a change in stimulation modality. We propose this can be addressed with a literature review and bench testing rather than a new clinical study.

### Q3 — Cardiac interlock as additional safety control

The NeurOne device includes a cardiac rhythm monitor interlock (safety MCU, independent of main processor) that automatically cuts stimulation within 100 ms of a > 15 BPM HR change. This is an additional safety control not present in the gammaCore predicate. Does FDA consider this a change that requires additional clinical safety data, or is it viewed as a safety enhancement that supports the SE argument?

*NeurOne position:* The cardiac interlock is a conservative safety addition responding to the known baroreceptor reflex risk (present in the predicate's labelling). We expect FDA to view this as a safety enhancement. We do not believe additional clinical data is required beyond the FAI-CV02 bench test demonstrating cutoff latency ≤ 100 ms.

### Q4 — Human factors engineering

Does FDA expect full human factors summative testing for the cervical VNS accessory, given that it is an accessory to the NeurOne Pro (T2) platform for which a separate 510(k) will be submitted? Or can the HFE programme be integrated into the platform-level T2 submission?

*NeurOne position:* We propose conducting HFE formative and summative testing at the platform level (T2 submission) and including cervical VNS-specific tasks (electrode placement, session initiation, response to cardiac alert) within the platform HFE programme. We request FDA's agreement that a separate standalone HFE submission for the cervical VNS accessory is not required.

### Q5 — Predicate clinical data applicability

The gammaCore clinical programme (K163334: n=150 cluster headache RCT; K173323: migraine study) used up to 24 mA and a fixed 25 Hz waveform. The NeurOne device operates at ≤ 2 mA and configurable 1–25 Hz. Does FDA agree that the gammaCore clinical data is applicable to the NeurOne device given its substantially lower current range? Alternatively, what clinical data does FDA recommend to establish safety and effectiveness for the NeurOne operating range?

*NeurOne position:* We propose that a focused tolerability study in 3 healthy adults (FAI-CV03, equivalent to what is described in NP-FW-CVNS-001 Rev 1) is sufficient to confirm safety at the NeurOne operating range, combined with literature support for the gammaCore programme at the overlapping 25 Hz frequency.

## 7. Labelling Concepts

### 7.1 Indications for use statement (proposed)

*"The NeurOne Pro Cervical VNS Accessory is indicated for non-invasive transcutaneous stimulation of the cervical vagus nerve for: (1) acute treatment of pain associated with episodic cluster headache in adults; (2) adjunctive preventive treatment of episodic cluster headache in adults; (3) acute treatment of migraine without aura in adults."*

### 7.2 Contraindications (proposed, consistent with gammaCore labelling)

- Active implanted electronic device (pacemaker, ICD, cochlear implant, drug pump)
- Carotid artery disease (stenosis, carotid endarterectomy within 3 months)
- Cervical vagotomy
- Pregnancy (precautionary)
- Age < 18 years

### 7.3 Warnings (proposed)

- Cardiac monitoring: safety MCU monitors cardiac rhythm during stimulation; device will automatically stop if heart rate changes by more than 15 BPM during the 5-second observation window. If cutoff occurs, do not restart without medical consultation.
- Do not use while driving or operating heavy machinery.
- Do not use over broken or irritated skin.
- Discomfort at stimulation site is expected and reversible; reduce current level if discomfort is excessive.
- Keep gel electrodes away from eyes and mucous membranes.

## 9. Relationship to T2 Platform 510(k)

The NeurOne Pro (T2) platform requires separate 510(k) submissions for each modality with FDA-regulated claims. The cervical VNS accessory 510(k) (this document) covers the tcVNS modality. The T2 platform's TMS and clinical tACS modalities will be filed under separate 510(k)s using their own modular predicates (docs/reference/regulatory-strategy.md):

| T2 modality | Primary predicate | 510(k) |
|------------|------------------|--------|
| Cervical VNS (tcVNS) | electroCore gammaCore K163334 + K173323 | This document |
| TMS | NeuroStar K083538, BrainsWay K122288 | Separate filing |
| Clinical tACS | Soterix K142485, Neuroelectrics K173185 | Separate filing |

NeurOne will request that FDA consider bundling the Q-Sub for all three modalities into a single multi-modality Type B meeting to reduce FDA resource burden.
